# Hairstyle feature research

## Editorial scope

The feature should help someone name a haircut or styling service and prepare for a consultation. It should not promise that a style is “for” one curl family, ethnicity, gender, face shape, or density. Length, density, shrinkage, growth pattern, previous chemical/heat work, scalp comfort, time, and maintenance can change the result. The 1A–4C code is a pattern vocabulary, not a suitability gate.

## Verified sources and findings

- [Andis, “Fluid Vol 1: Low Taper Fade”](https://andis.com/BarberStylistEducation/VideoDetail?EduItemID=84) — Andis describes a low taper fade with longer sides and top and a transition connected through the parietal ridge. Use this as an example of a low taper-fade shape, not a universal definition of every taper.
- [Andis, “Soft & Subtle Buzz Cut”](https://andis.com/BarberStylistEducation/VideoDetail?EduItemID=1439) — Andis educator Hayden Cassidy demonstrates a soft/subtle buzz using clippers and presents it as a low-maintenance short haircut. The page supports treating “buzz cut” as a family with finish variations, not one fixed silhouette.
- [Wahl Professional, “STEP-BY-STEP GUIDE FOR CUTTING HAIR”](https://www.wahlpro.com/amfile/file/download/file/504/product/582/) — Wahl’s guide describes a bald fade as virtually bald at the bottom, fading to slightly longer hair on top, and separately describes a bald cut as progressively shorter all over. It also shows that guard choice, direction, blending, and tapered ear guards materially affect the result.
- [Milady, “Milady Standard Natural Hair Care and Braiding, 2nd Edition”](https://www.milady.com/catalog/milady-standard-natural-hair-care-braiding) — the publisher’s procedure list keeps Flat Twists, Two-Strand Twist, Starting Locs, Cultivating and Grooming Locs, and multiple braid services as separate procedures. Treat those as distinct service labels; do not collapse every rope-like style into “twists.”
- [Carol’s Daughter, “Braids vs. Twists: What’s The Difference?”](https://carolsdaughter.com/blogs/beauty-blog/braids-vs-twists-what-s-the-difference) — the brand’s own styling explanation distinguishes braids as three sections woven over and under from twists as two sections wrapped together. This is commercial styling guidance, not medical evidence; exclude its claims about lower tension, best hair types, duration, or product benefits from the feature’s factual model.
- [American Academy of Dermatology, “Hairstyles that pull can lead to hair loss”](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/hair-loss/causes/hairstyles) — AAD notes that overly tight braids, locs, cornrows, and other pulling styles can contribute to traction alopecia; pain, stinging, crusts, or tenting are warning signs. Keep the caution brief and direct users with persistent hair loss to a board-certified dermatologist.

## Domain distinctions for the content model

### Taper, fade, and buzz

- **Taper:** a gradual shortening concentrated around selected edges, commonly the sideburns and/or neckline, while more length remains elsewhere. Always ask where the taper sits and how high it should rise.
- **Fade:** a blend that travels farther around the head and may finish at shadow, skin, or bald. “Low,” “mid,” “high,” “drop,” and “skin/bald” communicate placement and finish; local shop vocabulary varies.
- **Taper fade:** a blended look combining a taper-focused edge with fade language. Confirm the exact perimeter and blend height rather than assuming the phrase means one fixed cut.
- **Buzz cut:** a clipper-led short cut whose length may be uniform or intentionally varied. Ask for the guard or approximate length, whether the top matches the sides, and how the neckline and sideburns should be finished.

### Twists, braids, and locs

- **Two-strand twist:** two sections wrapped around each other; commonly worn as individual hanging twists or used as a temporary set. Confirm part size, length, and whether extensions are involved.
- **Flat twist:** a two-strand technique worked along the scalp, so it reads as a scalp-following row rather than a free-hanging twist.
- **Rope twist:** a shop-dependent label. It may describe a tighter two-strand rope effect or a technique where each section is twisted before the sections are wrapped together. Ask the stylist to show a reference and clarify whether the service is individual or scalp-based.
- **Locs:** intentionally formed and maintained locked strands. Starting or cultivating locs is a different service from a temporary two-strand twist; ask about the starter method, parting, maintenance interval, and whether the goal is to keep or remove the locked form.
- **Braids:** an interlaced service family distinct from twists in the consultation vocabulary. Ask whether the requested look is box, cornrow, stitch, knotless, or another braid method, and whether extensions are used.

## Recommended page structure

1. **Plain-language summary:** what the style changes (edge, length, shape, or arrangement).
2. **Name variants:** the terms a client may hear, with a note that shop vocabulary varies.
3. **Ask your barber/stylist:** placement, shortest finish, length/guard, part size, extensions, neckline, and maintenance.
4. **What changes the result:** length, density, shrinkage, growth pattern, prior processing, and time/maintenance—described as variables, not exclusions.
5. **Comfort and scalp note:** for tight twists, braids, and loc styles, the style should not hurt; persistent pain or hair loss warrants professional medical advice.
6. **Neighbor links:** related styles such as low taper → low fade, buzz → bald cut, two-strand twist → flat twist → locs.

## Imagery limits

Use original diagrams or clearly labeled illustrative references to show placement, taper height, guard-length progression, parting, and twist direction. A generated styled image cannot establish the subject’s exact natural 1A–4C type, strand thickness, density, porosity, ethnicity, or suitability for the style. Do not use one photorealistic model as the definition of a style; show variation and label images as illustrative rather than diagnostic.
